
2. Methods

2.1 Compound Selection and Docking Pose Extraction

Three natural product hits identified through the virtual screening and molecular
docking pipeline (described in Papers 1 and 2) were selected for molecular dynamics
simulation: CNP0286261 (African NP, AutoDock Vina score -11.86 kcal/mol),
CNP0275186 (African NP, -11.13 kcal/mol), and CNP0539885 (Global NP,
-10.03 kcal/mol). Pyrimethamine, the clinical antifolate whose resistance is
modelled by the K1 strain quadruple mutant (N51I/C59R/S108N/I164L), was included
as a positive reference control (docking score -6.90 kcal/mol). Best-mode docked
poses were extracted from AutoDock Vina PDBQT output files and converted to PDB
format using RDKit (version 2024.03.6) by stripping AutoDock-specific atom type
columns and retaining only standard PDB coordinate fields.

2.2 System Preparation

The receptor structure (PDB: 1J3I, chain A, DHFR domain) was prepared using
GROMACS 2023.3 pdb2gmx with the AMBER99SB-ILDN force field. Ligand parameters
were generated using ACPYPE (version 2022.7.21) with the General AMBER Force
Field version 2 (GAFF2) and AM1-BCC partial charges computed via AmberTools 23
antechamber. Protein-ligand complexes were assembled by combining the processed
receptor structure with the energy-minimised docked ligand coordinates.

Each system was solvated in a rectangular TIP3P water box with 1.0 nm padding
using gmx solvate, with explicit box dimensions of 4.526 x 6.387 x 4.703 nm
derived from the complex structure. Sodium (Na+) and chloride (Cl-) ions were
added to a physiological concentration of 0.15 M using gmx genion, with nine
chloride ions added to neutralise the net +9e protein charge. Final system sizes
were approximately 13,480-13,490 atoms per compound.

2.3 Energy Minimisation and Equilibration

All systems underwent steepest-descent energy minimisation until the maximum
force converged below 1,000 kJ/mol/nm. Temperature equilibration (NVT) was
performed for 100 ps at 310 K using the V-rescale thermostat with a coupling
constant of 0.1 ps, with position restraints applied to protein heavy atoms.
Pressure equilibration (NPT) was performed for 100 ps at 1 bar using the
Berendsen barostat with a coupling constant of 2.0 ps and compressibility of
4.5 x 10^-5 bar^-1. The Berendsen barostat was selected for NPT equilibration
to ensure pressure stability; the Parrinello-Rahman barostat was applied during
production MD to sample the correct NPT ensemble.

Electrostatic interactions were computed using the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME)
method with a real-space cutoff of 1.0 nm, a Fourier grid spacing of 0.16 nm,
and fourth-order interpolation. Van der Waals interactions used a cutoff of
1.0 nm with the Verlet neighbour list scheme. All bonds involving hydrogen atoms
were constrained using the LINCS algorithm with fourth-order expansion and one
LINCS iteration.

2.4 Production Molecular Dynamics

Production MD simulations were performed for 100 ns for each hit compound and
50 ns for the pyrimethamine reference, using a 2 fs integration timestep.
Simulations were executed using GROMACS 2023.3 compiled with CUDA acceleration
on an NVIDIA T4 GPU (Google Colab Pro). Non-bonded interactions were computed
on the GPU (-nb gpu) while PME electrostatics and coordinate updates were
handled on the CPU (-pme cpu -update cpu) to maintain optimal memory usage.
Trajectories were saved at 10 ps intervals, yielding 10,010 frames per 100 ns
simulation and 5,005 frames for the pyrimethamine reference.

2.5 Trajectory Post-Processing

Raw trajectories from chunked production runs were concatenated using gmx trjcat
and subjected to periodic boundary condition (PBC) correction using gmx trjconv
with protein-centred wrapping (-center -pbc mol -ur compact) prior to all
analyses. This procedure removes translational drift and re-images the protein
and ligand into the primary unit cell, ensuring physically meaningful
inter-atomic distances throughout the trajectory.

2.6 Structural Stability Analysis

All trajectory analyses were performed using MDAnalysis version 2.10.0 in
Python 3.11. Backbone root mean square deviation (RMSD) was computed relative
to the initial frame after alignment on backbone atoms (C, CA, N, O) spanning
the full simulation length. The first 10 ns were treated as the equilibration
period and excluded from convergence statistics; mean RMSD and standard
deviation were reported for post-equilibration frames. Per-residue root mean
square fluctuation (RMSF) was computed for C-alpha atoms over post-equilibration
frames (10-100 ns), with particular attention to the four resistance mutation
sites: N51I (residue 51), C59R (residue 59), S108N (residue 108), and
I164L (residue 164). Radius of gyration was computed for all protein heavy atoms
at 10-frame (0.1 ns) intervals as a measure of overall protein compactness.

2.7 Hydrogen Bond Analysis

Ligand-protein hydrogen bonds were identified using MDAnalysis
HydrogenBondAnalysis with a donor-acceptor distance cutoff of 3.5 A and a
donor-hydrogen-acceptor angle cutoff of 120 degrees. Analysis was performed
over post-equilibration frames subsampled at 10-frame intervals (0.1 ns
resolution) to reduce computational cost while retaining statistical
representativeness. Hydrogen bond occupancy was defined as the percentage of
analysed frames in which each unique donor-acceptor pair formed a hydrogen bond.
Mean hydrogen bonds per frame was computed as the total number of hydrogen bond
events divided by the number of analysed frames.

2.8 Ligand-Protein Contact Frequency Analysis

Ligand-protein residue contacts were computed over post-equilibration frames
subsampled at 50-frame (0.5 ns) intervals. A contact was defined as any
ligand heavy atom within 4.0 A of any atom of a given protein residue. Contact
frequency was expressed as the percentage of analysed frames in which each
residue maintained contact with the ligand. Only residues with contact frequency
greater than 5% were retained for analysis and visualisation. Residues shared
between each hit compound and the pyrimethamine reference were identified to
assess binding pocket overlap.

2.9 Representative Frame Extraction and Binding Site Visualisation

A representative equilibrated binding conformation was extracted from each
trajectory for structural comparison with the initial docked poses. The
representative frame was defined as the frame within the final 20 ns of
simulation whose backbone RMSD (relative to the initial frame) was closest to
the mean RMSD of that period. This selection criterion ensures the chosen
structure reflects the predominant equilibrated binding mode rather than a
transient conformation.

Binding site figures were generated using PyMOL open-source version 3.1.0.
Two sets of figures were produced per compound: the initial docked pose
(from AutoDock Vina, described in Paper 2) and the MD-equilibrated representative
frame. In all figures, the protein is shown as a semi-transparent cartoon,
binding site residues within 5 A of the ligand are shown as lines, key
hydrogen bond partner residues are shown as sticks, and the four resistance
mutation residues are highlighted in magenta with CA atom labels. Ligands are
shown as sticks with oxygen and nitrogen atoms represented as spheres.
All figures were ray-traced at 300 DPI with a white background.

2.10 Software and Reproducibility

All simulations and analyses were performed using open-source software:
GROMACS 2023.3, MDAnalysis 2.10.0, RDKit 2024.03.6, ACPYPE 2022.7.21,
AmberTools 23, PyMOL open-source 3.1.0, and pyedr. Input files, MDP parameters,
topology files, representative frame PDB files, PyMOL visualisation scripts,
and all analysis scripts are available at:
https://github.com/KennethOdoh157/pfdhfr-inhibitor-discovery
