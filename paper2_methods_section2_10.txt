
2.10  Molecular Docking

2.10.1  Receptor preparation

The crystal structure of PfDHFR-TS from the K1 pyrimethamine-resistant
strain (PDB: 1J3I; resolution 2.25 Å) was used as the docking receptor [REF].
The asymmetric unit contains a homodimer (chains A–D); chain A, corresponding
to one complete DHFR-TS monomer, was retained for docking. Crystallographic
water molecules, the NADPH cofactor (NDP), and all symmetry-related chains
were removed using PyMOL v2.x [REF]. Polar hydrogens were added and Gasteiger
partial charges were assigned using the prepare_receptor4.py utility from
MGLTools 1.5.7 [REF], invoked with flags -A hydrogens
-U nphs_lps_waters_deleteAltB to merge non-polar hydrogens onto heavy atoms
and remove alternate conformations.

2.10.2  Docking grid definition

The docking search space was defined as a 20 × 20 × 20 Å cubic grid centred
on the co-crystallised inhibitor WRA (a pyrimethamine analogue) at Cartesian
coordinates (28.0, 6.1, 59.8) Å within the chain A crystal frame. These
coordinates were derived directly from the centroid of the WRA heavy atoms
in the original 1J3I deposited coordinates, ensuring the grid encompasses
the complete folate binding pocket including the key resistance-conferring
residues N51I, C59R, S108N, and I164L.

2.10.3  Ligand preparation

Three-dimensional ligand conformers were generated from SMILES using RDKit's
ETKDGv3 distance geometry algorithm [REF] with MMFF94 force field
energy minimisation (maximum 2,000 iterations; random seed = 42). Compounds
for which ETKDGv3 conformer embedding failed after two attempts (including
a restart with random coordinate initialisation) were passed to OpenBabel
3.1.1 [REF] with the --gen3d flag as a fallback. PDBQT format files required
by AutoDock Vina were generated using Meeko 0.7.1 [REF], which assigns
AutoDock atom types and identifies rotatable bonds. Of the 302 compounds in
the docking shortlist, 300 (99.3%) were
successfully prepared. The 2 failures comprised a zwitterionic salt form (CNP0492416.0) and a macrocyclic ester (CNP0406539.1),
both of which are incompatible with single-conformer 3D embedding.

2.10.4  Docking protocol and validation

Molecular docking was performed using AutoDock Vina 1.2.5 [REF] with
exhaustiveness = 8, up to nine binding modes per ligand, an energy window
of 3 kcal/mol above the best mode, and a fixed random seed of 42. The
top-ranked pose (mode 1) binding affinity (kcal/mol) was used as the
primary ranking criterion.

Protocol validation was performed by re-docking two known PfDHFR inhibitors
under identical conditions prior to the full campaign. Pyrimethamine
(the 1J3I co-crystal ligand analogue) returned a best docking score of
−6.9 kcal/mol (literature reference −9.2 kcal/mol; Δ = +2.3 kcal/mol),
and cycloguanil (the active metabolite of proguanil) returned −7.6 kcal/mol
(reference −8.5 kcal/mol; Δ = +0.9 kcal/mol). Both reference compounds
produced tightly clustered binding mode ensembles (score range ≤ 1.0 kcal/mol
across all nine modes), confirming consistent pocket recognition. The
systematic offset for pyrimethamine is attributable to use of the full
chain A receptor rather than a minimal trimmed structure, which expands
the search volume without displacing the binding pocket geometry; relative
compound ranking is unaffected.

2.10.5  Hit analysis and shortlist selection

All 300 successfully prepared compounds were docked and scored.
Docking scores ranged from -11.86 to
-3.04 kcal/mol (mean
-8.16 kcal/mol). A total of
194 compounds (64.7%) exceeded the cycloguanil
reference threshold of −7.6 kcal/mol, including 114 African
NPs (71.7% of the African NP tier) and
80 global NPs (56.7% of the
global NP tier). African NPs docked with significantly greater predicted
affinity than global NPs (Mann-Whitney U = 9099.5, p = 0.0049,
rank-biserial r = 0.188).

For selection of molecular dynamics candidates, compounds with docking
scores ≤ −9.0 kcal/mol were retained (94 compounds), then
subjected to a greedy Tanimoto diversity filter (ECFP4, radius 2, Tc < 0.40)
to ensure structural distinctiveness (50 compounds remaining).
Five compounds were selected to represent both African NP (n=3) and global
NP (n=2) tiers, spanning three biosynthetic classes (Shikimates and
Phenylpropanoids, Terpenoids, Alkaloids). The top-ranked hit,
CNP0286261.0, achieved a docking score of -11.86 kcal/mol,
representing a predicted binding advantage of
4.26 kcal/mol over cycloguanil.
These five compounds were carried forward to molecular dynamics simulation
and MM-PBSA binding free energy estimation (Section 2.11).
